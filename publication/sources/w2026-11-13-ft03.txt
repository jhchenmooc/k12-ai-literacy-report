Participants were recruited in collaboration with an elementary school in an urban area in western Germany. The school provides regular computer instruction, but AI was not part of the curriculum, so children’s prior experience with AI stemmed from outside of school. The final sample included 15 girls and 15 boys from four third- and fourth-grade classes.

Passage (Limitations and future work, paragraph 2 (PDF p. 49)):
Because no data on the children’s ethnocultural or socioeconomic backgrounds were collected, the findings cannot contextualize differential access to AI-related experiences and adult guidance.
Passage (Limitations and future work, paragraph 3 (PDF p. 49)):
Although the interview guide was piloted and no signs of fatigue were observed, an influence of interview length on individual responses cannot be ruled out. In addition, the single information-seeking task foregrounded the system’s informational ability, so judgments of benevolence and integrity may be context-dependent and should be examined across varied interaction scenarios.
Passage (Limitations and future work, paragraph 4 (PDF p. 49)):
A further limitation concerns the design of the study system. The LLM was deliberately given a human name to foster a natural interaction. This choice may have contributed to the anthropomorphic responses observed, and, within the present design, its specific effect cannot be isolated from that of other social cues, such as natural language use and emojis.
