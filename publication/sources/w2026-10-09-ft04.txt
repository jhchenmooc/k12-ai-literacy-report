Methods excerpt: Schools were selected through convenience sampling based on accessibility and the willingness of school principals to participate. … Within this framework, the quantitative component employed a correlational design to examine the relationship between children’s knowledge of ChatGPT and their English language achievement. … The study comprised 255 third-grade children (aged 7–8 years) from 15 elementary schools in the Eastern Region of Saudi Arabia. … From each school, one class was randomly selected to enhance representativeness. … In addition to the quantitative sample, a subsample of 50 children was selected for qualitative interviews using convenience sampling. … Young children’s English as a Second Language (ESL) achievement was assessed using their cumulative grade point average (CGPA) in English from the previous semester. … The questionnaire measured knowledge across five domains: general knowledge, applied knowledge, awareness of risks and limitations, ethical knowledge, and self-evaluation and confidence in using ChatGPT.

Results excerpt: Table 3 indicates that children’s overall knowledge of ChatGPT is at a moderate level (M = 2.79, SD = 0.52). … A relative strength is observed in both general and practical/application knowledge compared to the other domains, whereas knowledge of risks and limitations, ethical awareness, and self-assessment and confidence are notably lower. … The strongest relationships were observed for general knowledge and practical/application knowledge, followed by self-assessment and confidence, whereas weaker relationships were identified for knowledge of risks, limitations, and ethical considerations. … Overall, the findings indicate that ChatGPT knowledge is associated with differences in English language achievement; however, no causal relationship can be inferred from the present study.

Limitations excerpt: First, the cross-sectional design limits the ability to determine the directionality of the relationships between ChatGPT knowledge and English language achievement. … In addition, several potential confounding variables may have influenced the observed associations. … These include socioeconomic status, parental support, access to digital devices and internet connectivity, prior digital literacy experiences, and differences in school resources and instructional quality. … Therefore, caution is warranted when extending these findings to other age groups, educational levels, or different cultural and institutional settings. … Specifically, exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) were not conducted due to the limited pilot sample size (N = 20), which does not meet the minimum requirements for stable and interpretable factor solutions.
