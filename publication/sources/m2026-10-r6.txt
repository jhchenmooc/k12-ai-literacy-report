Context and sample excerpt (PDF p. 5, Context and research participants):
This study was conducted in the Chinese EFL context at the primary education level and during the summer holiday in August 2025. Our research participants were recruited via the authors’ network using a convenience sampling approach until a sufficient sample size was achieved … Fifty participants were included, comprising 25 pairs of young learners (age: 7–11 years) and parents. … Nevertheless, we regard these profiles as exploratory and do not claim generalisability beyond similar contexts. Regarding the parents’ English proficiency, 20 of 25 (80%) reported possessing only basic knowledge of English or beginner-level skills. Furthermore, 20 of 25 parents (80%) expressed that they had never used AI chatbots to support their children’s informal learning of English.

Game design excerpt (PDF pp. 4–5, Design and development of the game):
Specifically, we incorporated a generative AI chatbot powered by a low-cost large language model, ERNIE-48 K … A total of five AI-supported characters were developed … Second, the AI-supported characters were designed to provide a rating and evaluative feedback. The rating (1 = lowest, 5 = highest) was assigned based on spoken English assessment criteria … Second, we applied RAG to ground the AI responses in the provided dataset, such as vocabulary lists and sample sentences related to small animals

Procedure excerpt (PDF pp. 5–6, Interventions):
The interventions were conducted in the participants’ preferred locations (e.g., their homes). … The parents were encouraged to guide their children throughout gameplay and to offer support when necessary. The planned gameplay duration was approximately 30 min.

Data excerpt (PDF p. 6, Data collection and analysis; PDF p. 9):
The data were collected from four sources: (1) video recordings of the participants’ engagement with the game, (2) learner–AI dialogues, (3) learners’ pre- and post-tests, and (4) semi-structured interviews with the learners and their parents. … To address RQ2, a 10-minute pre- and post-test were administered to evaluate the learners’ vocabulary knowledge and speaking skills. … Each test had a maximum score of 50 marks

This pattern indicates that the learners in Cluster 1 had higher prior English proficiency than those in Cluster 2, which needs to be taken into account when interpreting subsequent differences in interaction patterns and learning gains between the clusters.

Findings excerpt (PDF p. 1, Abstract):
Two distinct learner–parent–AI interaction profiles were identified. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test indicated significant increases in scores from pre-test to post-test within both profiles. However, learners’ post-test scores did not differ significantly across the learner profiles after controlling for their pre-test scores. The regression analysis results indicated that learners’ pre-test scores, their engagement in knowledge-learning tasks, and parental cognitive engagement strategies were positively associated with learners’ post-test scores. By contrast, learner–AI interaction types showed no significant association with learners’ post-test scores.

Parent strategies excerpt (PDF p. 14, Parents' verbal instructions):
The parents in both clusters used strategies such as prompting and questioning, cueing, and answer providing, while parents in Cluster 1 also used corrective feedback.

Perceptions excerpt (PDF p. 16, RQ3):
The reported benefits of the AI-supported characters can be categorised into three main sub-themes, namely facilitating speaking skills, supporting vocabulary acquisition, and providing evaluative feedback. … Several suggestions were also identified, namely simplifying AI feedback, including bilingual AI feedback, and providing scaffolded prompts.

Limitations excerpt (PDF pp. 20–21, Conclusion and limitations):
First, this study focused on one game scenario for informal learning of English. … the study did not include a control or comparison group. … Finally, this study involved a small number of participants, comprising 25 EFL learners paired with 25 parents. … In addition, the stepwise regression was based on 25 cases. This condition may produce unstable coefficients, inflated model fit, and overfitting, even when residual and collinearity diagnostics are acceptable. The regression findings should therefore be understood as exploratory rather than robust or broadly generalisable predictive factors.
