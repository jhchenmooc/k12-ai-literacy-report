Methods excerpt: (§2.2 pp. 4–5; §2.3 p. 5–6; §2.4–2.5 p. 6) The study was conducted with elementary school (4th grade) and middle school (5th, 6th, 7th, and 8th grade) students enrolled in a province in Turkey. … The study sample consisted of 870 students who participated voluntarily and were selected through purposive sampling. … 48.7% (n = 424) of the participants are male, while 51.3% (n = 446) are female students. … An initial pool of 50 original items was developed by the researchers, with 10 items representing each preliminary domain. … Based on the expert opinions, the scale items were reorganized, and changes were made to the wording of two items. … The pilot study involved a total of 100 students, including 50 elementary school students and 50 middle school students. … No items were removed at this stage. … The developed scale items were prepared using a five-point Likert-type rating scale.

Results excerpt: (§3 Data analysis p. 7; §4 Discussion p. 13) The initial analysis primarily revealed a ten-factor structure. … After this initial screening, AFA was conducted again using the remaining 35 items. … EFA results revealed that the scale has a structure consisting of 6 factors and 22 items.

Limitations excerpt: (§5 Limitations and future research p. 15) First, the fact that the research sample consisted only of students studying in Niğde province and was formed using convenience sampling is a significant methodological constraint that limits the generalizability of the findings. … Second, the fact that EFA and CFA were performed on the same sample can be considered a methodological limitation. … Therefore, neither changes in AI literacy associated with age and educational experiences nor the temporal stability of the scale scores could be examined.
