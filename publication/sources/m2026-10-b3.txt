Guidebook excerpt (Abstract):
This study examined acceptance of the AI Learning Application Guidebook, developed in Elementary and Advanced editions for learners aged 9–12 and 13–18, respectively. The Elementary edition features scaffolded, platform-based, non-generative activities, whereas the Advanced edition includes authentic applications, ethical reasoning exercises, and supervised engagement with generative AI. The guidebooks were evaluated in Taiwanese classrooms by 831 participants (794 students and 37 teachers).
Sample excerpt (3.2 Participants; Table 1):
Teachers and students from elementary, junior high, and senior high schools across northern, central, southern, and eastern Taiwan participated in the implementation and feedback phase of the AI Learning Application Guidebooks. … Students Elementary (Grades 3-6) 274 34.5 Students Secondary (Grades 7-12) 520 65.5
Procedure excerpt (3.2 Participants):
Each reading session lasted about 30 minutes and was immediately followed by an online questionnaire assessing perceived usefulness, readability, engagement, and intended future use, together with an open-ended item inviting additional feedback on the guidebooks’ content and design. … They completed the acceptance questionnaire after classroom facilitation or preparatory reading.
Data collection excerpt (3.5 Data Collection and Analyses):
Data collection occurred in November 2025 during the inaugural large-scale deployment of the AI Learning Application Guidebooks on Taiwan’s Adaptive Learning Platform.
Instrument excerpt (3.4 Instruments):
The questionnaire included 4 items for PE, 4 items for EE, 5 items for PP, and 5 items for BI, resulting in a total of 18 closed-ended items, along with one open-ended question for additional feedback.

Design excerpt (3.3 Research Design):
The study adopted a quantitative, cross-sectional design to evaluate the usability and acceptance of the AI Learning Application Guidebook … the student dataset was randomly split into a development sample for Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) and a validation sample for Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA). … multi-group SEM compared Elementary and Advanced cohorts … Given the limited teacher sample size, teacher data were analyzed descriptively
Findings excerpt (Abstract; 5.2; 5.3):
Measurement invariance was supported across the two student editions … Qualitative findings highlighted participants’ perceived need for age-sensitive, multimodal, bilingual, and ethically explicit materials. … Latent-mean comparisons indicated higher levels of PE, EE, PP, and BI in the Elementary group than in the Advanced group. … these differences should be interpreted as edition-linked cohort patterns rather than as evidence of developmental change or isolated material effects. … the findings indicate that participants perceived the ethical themes as visible and relevant. They do not provide direct evidence of gains in ethical understanding, reasoning, awareness, or responsible AI competency.
Limitations excerpt (Abstract; 5.4):
The study did not measure AI literacy achievement, learning gains, actual adoption, or sustained use; therefore, the findings indicate short-term acceptance and perceived readiness for further use after initial guided exposure, not implementation effectiveness or learning outcomes. … The observed coefficients and cohort patterns may not generalize to systems with different curricula, governance arrangements, languages, or levels of teacher autonomy. … Common method variance could not be ruled out … The small teacher sample also limited cross-role analyses to exploratory checks
