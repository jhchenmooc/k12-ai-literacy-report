Sample excerpt (PDF p. 6, 2.2 Participants):
The study sample consisted of 183 Spanish Early Childhood and Primary Education teachers recruited through a non-probability convenience sampling strategy [65]. … To recruit participants, the research team contacted the leadership teams of schools located in different Spanish autonomous communities via institutional email. … Since the dissemination of the invitation was managed by the school leadership teams, the researchers could not determine the total number of teachers who received the questionnaire and, consequently, an exact response rate could not be calculated. … The final sample comprised 39 Early Childhood Education teachers and 144 Primary Education teachers. … Teachers from 15 Spanish autonomous communities participated in the study

Data excerpt (PDF p. 7, 2.3 Data-Collection Procedure):
Data were collected using an open-ended online questionnaire developed specifically for this study. … It also included a dichotomous item assessing self-reported familiarity with GAI and three open-ended questions: (1) “What barriers have you encountered when implementing GAI in your teaching practice?”; (2) “What ethical concerns do you have regarding the use of GAI in education?”; and (3) “How do you think GAI affects students’ learning?”.

Barriers excerpt (PDF p. 10 and p. 12, 3.1):
As shown in Figure 1, teacher training and digital competence (CA1; n = 95 coded references) was the most prominent barrier in the coded material. … Overall, the distribution of coded references identifies teacher training and digital competence as the most prominent theme, followed by implementation status, workload, and technical limitations.

Ethical concerns excerpt (PDF p. 12 and p. 14, 3.2):
The transformation of higher-order cognitive processes (CB2; n = 44 coded references) was the most prominent ethical theme. Within this category, the decline in critical thinking was the most frequently coded issue (n = 29) … Overall, the coded-reference distribution positions cognitive development, misuse, privacy, and information reliability as the most prominent ethical themes in the dataset.

Perceived impact excerpt (PDF p. 14 and p. 16, 3.3):
References to perceived negative impacts (CC2; n = 110) were more prominent than references to positive impacts (CC1; n = 83). … Half of the coded references within this category indicated that the impact of GAI depends on how it is used (n = 32).

Regarding RQ4, the results show greater concern among Early Childhood Education teachers than among Primary Education teachers regarding the potential negative effects of GAI on learning processes. … both Early Childhood and Primary Education teachers emphasized the importance of teacher training and digital competence in virtually identical terms.
