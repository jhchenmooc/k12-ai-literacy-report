Sample excerpt (PDF p. 1, Abstract):
A regionally stratified online survey was conducted with 768 in-service teachers (378 from secondary vocational schools, 390 from higher vocational colleges) across eastern, central, and western mainland China.

Procedure excerpt (PDF pp. 7–8, Participants and procedure):
This study employed a cross-sectional, self-reported online questionnaire design … we adopted a regionally stratified sampling frame covering the eastern, central, and western regions, combined with convenience sampling of cooperating institutions within each region. … Data were collected from March to April 2026 through Wenjuanxing, a widely used Chinese online survey platform. The questionnaire link was distributed via institutional academic affairs offices or teacher development centers at 14 cooperating institutions (6 SVSs and 8 HVCs) across nine provinces representing the three regions … A total of 812 questionnaires were returned, of which 768 (94.6%) were retained after applying the predefined data-cleaning rules … The final sample (N = 768) included 378 SVS teachers (49.2%) and 390 HVC teachers (50.8%)

Measures excerpt (PDF pp. 8–9, Measures):
AI teaching self-efficacy was measured with the TAICS [8], a scale originally developed and validated with K-12 teachers. The TAICS comprises 24 items distributed across six dimensions (4 items each): AI Knowledge (AIK … AI Assessment (AIA), AI Ethics (AIE), Human-Centered Education (HCE), and Professional Engagement (PEN). … all 24 items were lightly contextualized to reference skill instruction, practical training, and industry-relevant content while preserving the original six-dimensional structure and response format … Teachers’ AI anxiety in the teaching context was assessed with a 12-item instrument adapted from the AI Anxiety Scale [17] … Teachers’ occupational well-being was operationalized via the 8-item Teacher Subjective Well-Being Questionnaire (TSWQ) [9]

Profiles excerpt (PDF p. 1, Abstract):
Four distinct profiles emerged: Foundational Stage (21.4%), Technically Skilled but Ethically Cautious (21.4%), Pedagogically Engaged but Technically Constrained (25.7%), and Holistic Competence (31.6%).

Well-being excerpt (PDF p. 14, Profile differences in well-being):
Mean TSWQ scores increased monotonically across the four profiles … Notably, the Pedagogically Engaged Type reported significantly higher well-being than the Technically Skilled Type

Moderation excerpt (PDF p. 1, Abstract):
AI anxiety moderated the profile-to-well-being association only for the Holistic Competence profile … and it accounted for a small share of variance overall … an association we interpret cautiously given the cross-sectional design.

Predictors excerpt (PDF p. 13, Predictors of membership):
Higher AI tool-use frequency was the strongest and most consistent predictor of membership in a more developed profile … and a greater number of AI-training sessions likewise predicted the three more developed profiles … HVC teachers and central/western-region teachers were less likely than SVS and easternregion teachers, respectively, to belong to some of the more developed profiles

Limitations excerpt (PDF p. 18, Conclusion):
First, on design and measurement, the study is cross-sectional and relies on self-report, so the associations among profiles, AI anxiety, and well-being are concurrent rather than causal, and common method variance—though mitigated by Harman’s test, varied response formats, and response-style screening—cannot be excluded … the headline Holistic-profile finding rests partly on a sparse, extrapolated high-anxiety region and cannot, in cross-sectional composite data, be fully separated from a ceiling or regression-to-the-mean dynamic … the average variance extracted for the TSWQ facets (≈ 0.37) fell below the 0.50 criterion, and the adapted AI-anxiety scale functioned as a unidimensional composite … Fourth, on scope, the sample—though regionally stratified—was drawn from cooperating institutions in a single country, and the model omitted structural JD-R constructs (e.g., workload, autonomy, organizational and institutional support for AI adoption)
