Abstract excerpt (PDF p. 3, Abstract sentences 1-3, 5):
This paper examines how Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) in K-12 education interacts with teachers' Pedagogical Content Knowledge (PCK). … Using the Refined Consensus Model (RCM) as an analytical framework, we analysed how GenAI influences the three PCK domains: personal PCK (pPCK), collective PCK (cPCK), and enacted PCK (ePCK). … Guided by the research question "How can teachers and GenAI collaborate to develop and use PCK and pedagogical reasoning in K-12?", we analysed three researcher-constructed vignettes – hypothetical but plausible scenarios representing GenAI use in planning, classroom interaction, and assessment – to derive analytical propositions about the PCK-GenAI relationship. … We propose that GenAI can amplify teachers' PCK when critically evaluated, but poses risks for novice teachers whose pPCK is still developing.

RCM realms excerpt (PDF p. 6, Section 3.1):
The RCM identifies three distinct realms of PCK: collective PCK (cPCK), representing specialised professional knowledge shared amongst multiple educators; personal PCK (pPCK), comprising an individual teacher's cumulative knowledge and skills derived from teaching experiences and professional contributions; and enacted PCK (ePCK), the specific subset of knowledge utilised during planning, teaching, and reflecting on lessons and students’ progress.

Methodology excerpt (PDF p. 7, Section 4):
The vignettes that we developed are hypothetical but plausible scenarios; they are not accounts of observed classroom events, but analytical tools grounded in documented practice.

Vignette construction excerpt (PDF pp. 7-8, Section 4.1):
Eleven internationally recognised researchers and teacher educators, with expertise in educational technology and subject specialisms spanning science, history, computer science, and English education across primary, secondary, and tertiary contexts in the UK, New Zealand, Portugal, Norway, Ireland, Australia, the USA, and North Africa, collaborated to develop three vignettes. … Each vignette was drafted by a small subgroup using empirical literature, professional experience and classroom examples, then critiqued by the full team for plausibility, authenticity, and relevance until consensus was reached

Analytic procedure excerpt (PDF p. 8, Section 4.2):
Subgroups analysed how GenAI in each scenario interacted with RCM domains – content, pedagogical, and contextual knowledge, including PCK variants – identifying required teacher competencies and potential risks. … The full team then conducted two rounds of review to address omissions, inconsistencies, and alignment with the literature, refining the analysis to consensus.

Theoretical implications excerpt (PDF p. 19, Section 6.3):
Specifically, we propose that the RCM be extended to explicitly position GenAI as an active mediating agent within the learning context layer – distinct from other contextual factors by virtue of its generative, pseudo-authoritative character and its capacity to simulate aspects of cPCK without the community validation that defines it.

Limitations excerpt (PDF p. 21, Section 6.5):
As hypothetical constructions, they cannot replicate the full complexity of real-time classroom dynamics – particularly for ePCK; longitudinal ethnographic studies of actual teacher-GenAI interactions are needed to test and extend the analytical propositions developed here. … Their validation also rested on researcher judgement rather than external practitioner review, and future iterations would benefit from broader input from classroom teachers across a wider range of subject and cultural contexts. … Since the researchers who constructed the vignettes also conducted the analysis, there is an inherent risk of propositions reflecting our existing theoretical commitments; iterative whole-group review mitigated but cannot eliminate this risk. … The analysis focused on teachers' knowledge and reasoning; how students' own GenAI interactions shape pedagogical dynamics and influence teachers' ePCK remains beyond its scope and represents an important direction for future research. … Finally, the vignettes were developed at a point in time; the rapid pace of LLM development and the move toward hybrid AI models means that specific tool characteristics will continue to evolve, even if the analytical framework remains applicable.

Concluding remarks excerpt (PDF p. 22, Section 6.8):
Through vignette-based analysis using the RCM, we have argued that this relationship is neither straightforwardly enabling nor straightforwardly threatening: GenAI can amplify teachers' professional knowledge when engaged critically, yet it also introduces distinctive risks – particularly for novice teachers whose pPCK is still forming – and reconfigures the relationships between the RCM's core knowledge realms in ways that existing frameworks do not adequately capture. … The human teacher is not displaced in this account; rather, teacher expertise, contextual judgement, and critical engagement emerge as more essential than ever.
