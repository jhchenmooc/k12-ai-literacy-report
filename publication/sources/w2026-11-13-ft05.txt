Passage (Abstract, sentence 1 (PDF p. 1)):
This pilot study explores the integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) into primary STEM education in Sweden through a structured Teacher Professional Development (TPD) model.
Passage (Abstract, sentences 3–5 (PDF p. 1)):
Eleven teachers participated in a six-month TPD programme comprising workshops, collaborative discussions, and classroom implementations. The empirical material consisted primarily of audio-recorded workshop discussions, which were transcribed and analysed, supplemented by a post-intervention questionnaire. Data were analysed using thematic coding informed by the Refined Consensus Model (RCM) and the Digital Promise AI Literacy Framework.
Passage (Abstract, sentence 7 (PDF p. 1)):
Teachers reported opportunities for timesaving, differentiated instruction, and enhanced creativity, alongside challenges related to mathematical inaccuracies, unrealistic time estimates, and the need for contextual adaptation.
Passage (Method, Research Design (PDF p. 6)):
The pilot was conceptualised as a design-oriented study aimed at examining the feasibility and refinement needs of a workshop-based TPD model for integrating GenAI in primary STEM education.
Passage (Method, Participants and Recruitment, paragraph 1 (PDF p. 7)):
The initial request came from a group of primary school teachers seeking support in using GenAI in their teaching. Following this, a wider invitation was sent to primary teachers and school leaders within the region where the university is situated in central Sweden. The invitation specified that participating teachers should teach grades four to six (students aged 10–12 years) and focus on STEM subjects.
Passage (Method, Participants and Recruitment, paragraph 2 (PDF p. 7)):
In total, there were 11 primary school teachers that volunteered to participate in the TPD and the research study. Of these, eight were females and three were male teachers, all of them teaching all the STEM subjects in grade 4–6 (students aged 10–12 years). The teaching experiences varied from 2 to 35 years. Some of the teachers came from the same school and in one case, it was only one teacher participating from a school.
Passage (Method, TPD Structure and Activities, paragraph 1 (PDF p. 8)):
The TPD programme comprised four university-based workshops lasting 2.5–3 h each and facilitated by a science teacher educator and researcher (author 1) and a mathematics teacher and researcher (author 2).
Passage (Method, TPD Structure and Activities, paragraph 1 (PDF p. 8)):
Between workshops, they implemented classroom tasks that progressed from mathematics (fractions), to science, and finally to integrated mathematics and science within a STEM-oriented approach.
Passage (Method, Data Collection and Analysis, paragraph 1 (PDF p. 9)):
Thematic analysis followed Braun and Clarke’s (2006) six-phase approach.
Passage (Method, Data Collection and Analysis (PDF p. 10)):
The questionnaire was developed by the research team specifically for this pilot study. The questionnaire consisted of 14 items combining closed-response and open-ended questions.
Passage (Method, Data Collection and Analysis (PDF p. 10)):
Given the exploratory pilot nature of the study and the small sample size, the questionnaire was used descriptively as a complementary source for triangulation rather than as a validated measurement instrument.
