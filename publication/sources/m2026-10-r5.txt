Title and affiliation excerpt (p. 1):
Integration of ChatGPT to enhance English speaking skills among secondary school students in Pakistan … Superior University, Lahore, Pakistan

Design excerpt (p. 1, Abstract):
A pre-test-post-test control group experimental design was employed, involving fifty Grade 9 students from a public school who were randomly assigned to experimental and control groups. English-speaking proficiency was assessed before and after the intervention through monologue and dialogue speaking tests using the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR) rubric.

Sample excerpt (p. 5, 3.2 Participants, sampling, and sample size):
The selected sample comprised ninth-grade female students enrolled in the Science, Arts, and Computer Science streams at a public school in Sahiwal. The school was selected because it possessed a well-equipped IT laboratory that provided the facilities required to implement the ChatGPT-assisted learning module. … The study population comprised 310 ninth-grade students categorized into three academic strata: Science (150 students), Arts (70 students), and Computer Science (90 students). Students were systematically selected from an ordered list of roll numbers … The final sample consisted of fifty (50) participants who were randomly assigned to the experimental (n = 25) and control (n = 25) groups.

Group assignment excerpt (p. 8, 3.4.2 Group assignment):
Following the pre-test, participants were randomly assigned to either the experimental or the control group using a lottery method.

Instrument excerpt (p. 6, 3.3.1 CEFR scale and 3.3.2 SEPT):
Each speaking component (range, accuracy, fluency, interaction, and coherence) was assessed using CEFR descriptors and assigned a numerical score from 1 to 6 corresponding to proficiency levels A1 to C2. … The final version of the Spoken English proficiency test (SEPT) consisted of three parts with a total duration of approximately 10–13 min.

Assessment excerpt (pp. 7–8, 3.4.1 Pre-test administration):
A human–AI collaborative assessment approach was employed using CEFR-based rubrics and a structured marking scheme. … Students were then assessed individually using ChatGPT Voice Mode by completing the monologue and dialogue tasks. … ChatGPT generated real-time transcripts and evaluated the CEFR speaking components of range, accuracy, fluency and coherence. The assessment process was further supported by qualified language experts as part of the assessment quality-control process. In the monologue task, the interaction component was evaluated by the language experts using the CEFR descriptors and a Yes (1)/No (0) checklist … The experts were blinded to participants’ group allocation to minimize potential assessment bias.

Intervention excerpt (p. 8, 3.4.4 Intervention; p. 9):
The intervention was conducted over 12 weeks. The experimental group received the ChatGPT-assisted learning module, whereas the control group received conventional speaking instruction. The intervention was implemented during regular Grade 9 English classes and consisted of one structured 90-minute session per week, followed by guided ChatGPT-assisted speaking practice throughout the week. Each session followed a standardized instructional sequence comprising a 5-minute teacher briefing, 60 min of ChatGPT-assisted speaking practice using standardized prompts, and 25 min of collaborative learning activities, including role plays, pair work, and group discussions. … They interacted with ChatGPT through voice and text … using standardized prompts to practice the weekly speaking topics.

Analysis excerpt (p. 10, 3.5 Data analysis):
As the data were not normally distributed, the non-parametric Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare the speaking proficiency scores of the experimental and control groups.

Findings excerpt (p. 10, 4 Results; p. 11–12):
As shown in Table 1, learners in the experimental group demonstrated a clear progression from the A1–A2 proficiency levels in the pre-test to the B1–C1 levels in the post-test. In contrast, the control group showed only slight improvement and remained largely within the A1–B1 proficiency levels. … The experimental group obtained higher mean scores than the control group across all components.

Interpretation excerpt (pp. 12–13, 5 Discussion):
The notably large effect sizes observed in the present study require careful interpretation. These substantial effects may reflect the combined influence of the instructional features discussed above, the sustained implementation of the 12-week intervention, and the clear differences in the learning experiences of the experimental and control groups. … The relatively homogeneous sample, including participants from the same school with similar educational and demographic backgrounds, may also have reduced variability in the observed outcomes. In addition, the standardized CEFR-based scoring procedure, human–AI collaborative assessment approach, and consistent assessment conditions across the pre-test and post-test may have contributed to greater consistency in measurement. Therefore, the observed effect sizes should be interpreted within the specific context of the present study, including its instructional design, participant characteristics, and assessment procedure, rather than generalized to other educational settings. … However, because the present study did not directly examine these learning processes, these interpretations should be regarded as theoretically informed explanations rather than direct evidence of the mechanisms underlying the observed improvements in English-speaking proficiency.

Limitations excerpt (p. 14, 7 Limitations):
It was conducted with a limited sample of 50 female secondary school students from a single urban public school in Sahiwal, Pakistan, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other educational contexts. The study focused specifically on English-speaking proficiency, and the intervention lasted 12 weeks, which limits the ability to assess long-term effects. … Future research could involve a larger and more diverse sample, extend the duration of the intervention … Additionally, future studies may include a delayed post-test to examine the long-term retention of speaking gains
