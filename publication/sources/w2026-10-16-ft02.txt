Framework excerpt (pp. 7–8, Core dimensions of the framework and their relationships): Based on the framework development approach described above, this study proposes an AIC-K12 framework comprising Foundational Competency, Application Competency, and Ethical Norms. … This framework has been adopted as the AI competency standard for K-12 teachers by a provincial education authority in eastern China (Zhejiang Provincial Department of Education, 2025). … Application Competency represents the concrete manifestation of teachers’ ability to translate AI-related knowledge and skills into professional action and encompasses three scenarios: Teaching Practice, Educational Research, and Classroom Management. … Ethical Norms permeate the entire process of AI use and encompass three aspects: safety, fairness, and educational effectiveness.

Sample excerpt (p. 8, Methodology > Participants and sample): This study employed convenience sampling and conducted an online survey via the Wenjuanxing platform between December 2025 and February 2026. … The survey was coordinated by the Department of Education of Province Z and distributed through local educational administrative authorities across the province. … Participants included K-12 teachers from all 11 prefecture-level cities in Province Z, representing different educational stages, subject areas, and levels of teaching experience. … A total of 21,450 questionnaires were collected. … Data cleaning was primarily based on response time and response patterns. … After data cleaning, 18,295 valid questionnaires were retained, yielding a valid response rate of 85.29%.

Scale development excerpt (pp. 11–12, Development of the AICS-K12): Purposive sampling was used to recruit eight experts in educational technology or AI in education and ten K-12 teachers for semi-structured interviews. … Through theoretical deduction, item development, and iterative revision, an initial scale comprising 30 items was developed. … Based on these criteria, eight experts were invited to evaluate the content validity of the initial framework and items, with particular attention to the clarity, relevance, logical coherence, and adequacy of the dimension structure and item wording (Fernández-Gómez et al., 2020). … Ultimately, 26 items were retained to form the pilot scale. … A pilot test was conducted with 100 K-12 teachers representing different educational stages, levels of teaching experience, and subject areas. … Based on participant feedback, three items that were difficult to understand were removed, the wording of five items was revised, and the organization of the dimensions was optimized to reduce cognitive load, resulting in a 23-item scale for the formal survey. … The sample was randomly divided into two homogeneous subsamples.

Factor structure excerpt (p. 13, Results > Exploratory factor analysis): Ultimately, 22 items were retained (see Appendix 2), yielding five factors consistent with the theoretically specified framework and explaining 74.393% of the total variance. … Based on the conceptual content of the items, the five factors were identified as Foundational Competency, Ethical Norms, Teaching Practice, Educational Research, and Classroom Management. … Teaching Practice, Educational Research, and Classroom Management loaded strongly on Factor 1, which was labeled Application Competency; Foundational Competency loaded independently on Factor 2; and Ethical Norms loaded independently on Factor 3.

Reliability, validity and invariance excerpt (pp. 17, 19): In summary, the TAIC scale developed in this study demonstrates satisfactory reliability and validity. … Overall, the AICS-K12 achieved scalar invariance across all six grouping variables: gender, age, teaching experience, educational stage taught, subject taught, and school location.

Level and group differences excerpt (pp. 26–27, Discussion > Overall level and group differences): The findings indicate that the overall level of AIC-K12 was moderately high. … Teachers scored relatively higher on Foundational Competency and Ethical Norms but lower on scenario-based Application Competency, with Classroom Management showing the lowest score and the greatest individual variation. … Regarding individual characteristics, no significant gender difference was found in overall AIC-K12 scores, although female teachers scored higher on Educational Research and Ethical Norms. … Overall AIC-K12 scores declined with increasing age. … Regarding teaching contexts, primary school and secondary vocational school teachers scored higher than junior and senior secondary school teachers. … Comparisons across subject areas showed that Information Technology teachers had the highest scores, whereas Mathematics and Natural Sciences teachers scored relatively lower. … Regarding school location, teachers in urban areas scored higher than those in towns and rural areas. … Overall, these findings indicate statistical differences across teacher groups but do not establish the causes underlying these differences.

Limitations excerpt (p. 28, Limitations and future research directions): The three-dimensional framework developed in this study is intended to assess K-12 teachers’ competency to understand, apply, and regulate the use of AI in professional work scenarios and does not encompass related constructs such as AI self-efficacy and AI trust. … This study employed cross-sectional self-report data, which may be subject to common method bias. … The observed group differences therefore represent statistical associations only and should not be interpreted causally. … In addition, the sample was drawn from a specific educational context within a single country, and the cross-cultural applicability of the findings remains to be established.
