Sample excerpt (PDF pp. 11–12, 3.2 Participants; Abstract):
secondary school students enrolled in public and private institutions in the Casablanca-Settat region of Morocco … A convenience sampling approach was utilized, with the questionnaire distributed online … The quantitative sample comprised one hundred fifty (150) students … a qualitative sample consisting of twelve (12) students was purposefully selected based on their willingness to participate in online follow-up interviews … qualitative data were collected separately through online semi-structured interviews with 12 students

Sample characteristics excerpt (PDF p. 14, 4.1; PDF p. 19, 4.4.2):
The mean age was 16.59 years (SD = 1.062), ranging from 15 to 19 years … Common Core (n = 28), First Year Baccalaureate (n = 68), and Second Year Baccalaureate (n = 54)

Instruments excerpt (PDF p. 12, 3.3 Instruments):
a questionnaire consisting of twenty-nine (29) items … five-point Likert scale … Sections B and C examined students’ use of AI tools … perceived learning outcomes, motivation, self-regulated learning, digital literacy, and students’ perceived benefits and challenges associated with AI-assisted learning … a semi-structured online interview guide comprising ten open-ended questions

Data collection excerpt (PDF p. 12, 3.4; PDF p. 20, 4.5):
Data collection was conducted over a period of fourteen weeks … The survey was administered through Google Forms … written responses to ten open-ended questions completed by 12 students … All quotations were translated from French into English by the first author

Design excerpt (PDF p. 11, 3.1; PDF p. 13, 3.5; PDF p. 18, 4.4):
an explanatory sequential mixed-methods design … analysed using descriptive statistics and correlation analyses using SPSS software … The qualitative interview data were analysed using thematic analysis … independent-samples t-tests and one-way ANOVAs were conducted

Devices and tools excerpt (PDF p. 15, 4.2.1–4.2.2; Abstract):
relied on smartphones (74%) … Chatbots were the most frequently used tools … ChatGPT was the most frequently used AI tool, primarily accessed on students’ personal mobile devices

Themes excerpt (Abstract; PDF p. 20, 4.5):
Qualitative findings identified four major themes … perceived learning benefits (clearer explanations and reduced academic stress) … concerns about AI hallucinations in STEM subjects … the "laziness paradox," reflecting reduced cognitive effort associated with excessive reliance on AI … Six main themes were identified

Verification excerpt (PDF pp. 21–22, 4.5.3 Self-regulation and study strategies):
The most dominant strategy consists of internal verification using official course materials or prescribed textbooks … In mathematics, however, mistrust prevails

Ethics excerpt (PDF p. 23, 4.5.5 Ethics and teachers’ discourse):
the majority of respondents actively dichotomize the technology into either a legitimate cognitive scaffolding aid or an illegitimate vehicle for passive plagiarism

Limitations excerpt (PDF p. 29, 5.5 Limitations and future research):
may not fully reflect rural educational settings in Morocco or secondary education systems in other geographical territories … does not directly measure objective academic performance or incorporate real-time software usage analytics … precluding definitive conclusions regarding causal relationships between AI usage, self-regulation, and long-term cognitive growth
